Use Excel with your keyboard and a screen reader to do basic tasks in Excel. You'll learn how to open the app, create a new workbook to insert your data, create simple formulas, sort or filter your data, and add charts to show what your data means.

Enter your data
As you enter data to the sheet, you work with rows, columns, and cells. Cells are referenced by their location in the row and column on the sheet, so cell A1 is in the first row of column A. On a new sheet, cell A1 is the default selection.
To select an empty cell where you want to start entering your data, press the arrow keys. As you move to cells in the workbook, you hear the cell reference (row and column) and "Blank" if the cell is empty. If the cell has content, you hear the content and cell reference.

Create a simple formula
You can enter simple formulas to add, subtract, multiply, or divide the numbers in your sheet. You create a formula by combining cell references (for example, B4 or D6) that contain the numbers you want to calculate with the math operator.
Note
The operators are the plus sign (+) for addition, the minus sign (-) for subtraction, the asterisk (*) for multiplication, and the forward slash (/) for division.
Select the cell where you want to put the result of the formula. This is typically to the right of or below the numbers you're calculating.
Type an equal sign (=). An Excel formula always starts with the equal sign.

Open Excel
On your iPhone's home screen, drag one finger around the screen until you hear "Excel," and then double-tap the screen.
When you open Excel for iOS for the first time, you might need to sign in to your Microsoft account. For instructions, go to
Sign in at the first use

Tip
If you did not sign out of the Excel for iOS app the last time you used it, the app opens without prompting you to sign in.
Sign in to Excel
You can sign in to Excel for iOS the first time you open the app or later on. After you sign in to Excel, your account is also available in other Microsoft 365 apps. You can use Excel without signing in, too.
Sign in at the first use
When you open Excel for iOS for the first time, the app starts the initial setup, and then you hear "Use Excel on the go."
Flick right until you hear "Email or phone number, text field," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your email address or phone number. When you're done, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick left or right until you hear "Next, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Enter password, password, secure text field, required," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your password.
When you're done, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, Flick left or right until you hear "Sign in button," and then double-tap the screen.
If you hear "Don't miss anything," flick right until you hear the option you want, and then double-tap the screen.
Sign in after the first use
When you decide to make the most of your workbooks, you can sign in to Excel for iOS even after you've already used the app without a sign-in.
Open Excel.
Tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick left or right until you hear "Navigation pane, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right or left until you hear "Sign in button," and then double-tap the screen.
Tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick right or left until you hear "Email, phone, or Skype, text field, required," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your email address, phone number, or Skype name.
When you're done, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick left or right until you hear "Next, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Password, secure text field," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your password.
When you're done, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick left or right until you hear "Sign in, button," and then double-tap the screen.

Save your work
Excel for iOS automatically saves your work if you're signed in, but you can change the name of the file and select the save location to save a copy of a file to your phone, for example.
Save a copy of your file with a new name
In an Excel worksheet, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick right until you hear "File button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save a copy button," and then double-tap the screen. You hear "Save as, file name, text field." The on-screen keyboard opens.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type the new file name.
Once you're done, tap near the bottom of the screen with four fingers, flick left until you hear "Done," and then double-tap the screen to save the file with the new name.
Tip
If you are trying to save a file with a name that already exists, you hear "Replace file?" flick right until you hear "Cancel button" or "Replace button," depending on what you want to do. Double-tap the screen to confirm your choice.
Select where to save your file
In an Excel worksheet, tap near the top of the screen with four fingers, flick right until you hear "File button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save a copy button," and then double-tap the screen. You hear: "Save as, file name, text field."
Flick right until you hear the location where to save your file. If you want to save the copy to your phone, flick right until you hear "On my iPhone, button," and double-tap the screen.
In the location option you selected, flick left or right until you hear the possible folder or other sublocation you want, and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save button," and then double-tap to save the file in the selected location.
Tip
If you are trying to save a file with a name that already exists, you hear "Replace file?" Flick right until you hear "Cancel button" or "Replace button," depending on what you want to do. Double-tap the screen to confirm your choice.

Open Excel for Android
On your Android device's home screen, drag one finger around the screen until you hear "Apps," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Excel," and then double-tap the screen.
When you open Excel for the first time, you might need to sign in to your Microsoft account. For instructions, go to
Sign in at the first use
.
Tip
If you have turned on
Ok Google
voice detection in the
Google
app, you can say: "Ok Google, open Excel."
If you did not sign out of the Excel app the last time you used it, the app opens without prompting you to sign in.
Sign in to Excel
You can sign in to Excel the first time you open the app or later on. After you sign in to Excel, your account is also available in other Microsoft 365 apps. You can use Excel without signing in, too.
Sign in at the first use
When you open Excel for the first time, the app starts the initial setup, and then you hear: "Sign in to Microsoft account."
Flick right until you hear "Editing, edit box, email, phone, or Skype," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your email address, phone number, or Skype name. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Flick right or left until you hear "Next, button," and double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Editing password, edit box," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your password. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Flick right or left until you hear "Sign in, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Sign in after the first use
When you decide to make the most of your workbooks, you can sign in to Excel even after you've already used the app without a sign-in.
Open Excel.
Flick right until you hear "Sign in," and then double-tap the screen. You hear: "Sign in, Back button."
Flick right until you hear "Sign in button," and then double-tap the screen. The
Sign in

Flick right until you hear "Editing, edit box, Email, phone, or Skype," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your email address, phone number, or Skype name. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Flick left or right until you hear "Next, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Editing password, edit box," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type your password. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Flick right or left until you hear "Sign in, button," and then double-tap the screen.
Open a workbook
When you open Excel, you land on the
Recent
page. It lists your most recent workbooks. You can quickly open one of your most recent workbooks to continue where you left off. You can also navigate to and open an older workbook.
Open a recent workbook
Open Excel. If you're already working on a workbook and want to open another one, flick down-then-left.
You land on the
Recent
page. Drag one finger on the screen until you hear the file you want, and then double-tap the screen.
Open an older workbook
Open Excel. If you're already working on a workbook and want to open another one, flick down-then-left.
Flick left or right until you hear "Open tab," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear the location of the file and double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear the name of the file you want and double-tap the screen to open it.
Create a new workbook
Excel files are called workbooks. Each workbook contains sheets, typically called spreadsheets or worksheets. You can add as many sheets as you want to a workbook, or you can create new workbooks to keep your data organized.
Open Excel. If you're already working on a workbook and want to create a new one, flick down-then-left.
Flick left or right until you hear "New button," and then double-tap the screen. You hear: "New, Back button."
Flick right until you hear the name of the workbook template you want to use, and then double-tap the screen.
If you want to start a new workbook using a blank template without predesigned formatting, flick left or right until you hear "Blank workbook," and double-tap the screen.
Add a new worksheet to the workbook
In a workbook, drag your finger around the lower-right corner of the screen until you hear "Add sheet button," and then double-tap the screen.
Note
If you don't hear "Add sheet button," the sheets bar is hidden. To display the sheets bar, drag your finger around the lower-left corner of the screen until you hear "Sheets button," and double-tap the screen.
Enter your data
As you enter data in the sheet, you work with rows, columns, and cells. Cells are referenced by their location in the row and column on the sheet, so cell A1 is in the first row of column A. On a new sheet, cell A1 is the default selection.
On the worksheet, drag your finger around the screen until you find the cell you want to work on.
To open the context menu, double-tap the screen. You hear: "Cut button."
Flick right until you hear "Edit button," and then double-tap the screen. The on-screen keyboard is displayed on the bottom half of the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type the numbers or text that you want. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Drag your finger around the upper-right corner of the screen until you hear "Enter button," and then double-tap the screen.

On the worksheet, drag your finger around the screen to find the cell you want to work on, and then double-tap the screen to select the cell.
To close the context menu, flick down-then left.
Flick left or right until you hear "More options button," and then double-tap the screen. You hear the name of the currently selected tab.
Double-tap the screen, flick left until you hear "Home, tab menu," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Number format menu," and double-tap the screen. The
Number Format

Flick right until you hear the format you want such as
Currency

Tip
If a format option has a submenu, you hear, for example, "Number menu." To open the submenu, double-tap the screen.
Create a simple formula
You can create simple formulas to add, subtract, multiply, or divide your numbers.
On the worksheet, drag your finger around the screen to find the cell where you want to display the result of the formula.
To open the context menu, double-tap the screen. You hear: "Cut button."
Flick right until you hear "Edit button," and then double-tap the screen to open the on-screen keyboard.
To start creating your formula, first type an equal sign ( = ). An Excel formula always starts with the equal sign.
When you have typed the equal sign, type a combination of cell references (like B4 or D6) and math operators. Type the plus sign (+) for addition, the minus sign (-) for subtraction, the asterisk (*) for multiplication, or the forward slash (/) for division. For example, type =
B4+B5
, =
B4-B5
, =
B4*B5
, or =
B4/B5
.
Tip
To do quick calculations, instead of cell references, you can enter numbers in your formula, for example, =
20+10
, =
20-10
, =
20*10
, or =
20/10
.
Once you're done, drag your finger around the upper-right corner of the screen until you hear "Enter button," and then double-tap the screen. Excel runs the calculation and inserts the result to the selected cell.
Save your work
Excel automatically saves your work if you're signed in, but you can change the name of the file and select the save location, for example, to save a copy to your phone.
Rename your file
In an Excel file, flick right until you hear "More options menu," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save as button," and then double-tap the screen. The
Save as
page opens. The focus is on the file name text field.
Double-tap the screen. To clear the current file name, flick right until you hear "Clear button," and then double-tap the screen.
Use the on-screen keyboard to type the new file name. When you're done, flick down-then-left to close the on-screen keyboard.
Flick right until you hear "Save button," and then double-tap to save the file with the new name.
Tip
If you are trying to save a file with a name that already exists, you hear: "Replace file?" Flick right until you hear "Cancel button" or "Replace button," depending on what you want to do. Double-tap the screen to confirm your choice.
Select where to save your file
In an Excel file, flick right until you hear "More options menu," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save as button," and then double-tap the screen. The
Save as
page opens. The focus is on the file name text field.
Flick left until you hear the location where to save your file. If you want to save the file to your phone, flick left until you hear "This device," and double-tap the screen.
In the location you selected, flick left or right until you hear the possible folder or other sublocation you want, and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Save button," and then double-tap to save the file in the selected location.
Tip
If you are trying to save a file with a name that already exists, you hear: "Replace file?" Flick right until you hear "Cancel button" or "Replace button," depending on what you want to do. Double-tap the screen to confirm your choice.
Print your work
To print your worksheets, make sure you have a printer connected to your device.
In an Excel file, flick right until you hear "More options menu," and then double-tap the screen.
Flick right until you hear "Print," and then double-tap the screen. The
Print Options
page opens.
To use the default print options, flick right until you hear "Print, button," and then double-tap the screen. If you hear "Allow Excel to use an online service from Microsoft to prepare files for printing," flick right until you hear "Allow button," and double-tap the screen.
To select a printer, flick right until you hear "Drop-down list, Select a printer," and then double-tap the screen. Flick right until you hear the name of the printer you want, and double-tap the screen to select.
To print, flick right until you hear "Print button," and then double-tap the screen.
For instructions on how to perform more advanced printing tasks, go to

Technical support for customers with disabilities
Microsoft wants to provide the best possible experience for all our customers. If you have a disability or questions related to accessibility, please contact the
Microsoft Disability Answer Desk
for technical assistance. The Disability Answer Desk support team is trained in using many popular assistive technologies and can offer assistance in English, Spanish, French, and American Sign Language. Please go to the Microsoft Disability Answer Desk site to find out the contact details for your region.
If you are a government, commercial, or enterprise user, please contact
the enterprise Disability Answer Desk
.
